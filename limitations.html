Limitations and Disclosures

This page lists every material limitation of the strategy and the methodology used to test it. Honest disclosure is not optional, the strategy’s claim to be a defensible piece of work depends on the writeup being explicit about what it does NOT promise.

Headline-result limitations

1. Selection bias on the 4-instrument basket

The headline basket (AAPL + MSFT + WMT + GLD) was selected from a tested universe of 12 instruments, 3 ETFs (SPX, TLT, GLD) and 9 single-stocks (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, JNJ, KO, PG, WMT, JPM, PEP). Selection was made post-hoc on the criterion of positive excess Sharpe in OOS halts_only mode. This is selection bias.

Mitigation: Deflated Sharpe Ratio with implied-independent-trials correction. The 12 raw trials have an average pairwise return correlation of 0.261, giving an implied independent-trials count of N̂ = 9 per Eq. 9 of the Bailey & López de Prado framework. The DSR (PSR) of the headline basket is 1.0000, meaning the headline excess Sharpe of +0.359 is statistically significant against the noise floor of 9 independent trials. PBO via CSCV is 0.0402 (well below the pre-committed 0.30 acceptance threshold).

The selection is acknowledged and quantitatively corrected, not hidden.

2. Risk-free rate assumption

All Sharpe ratios reported as “excess” use a 2.33% annualized risk-free rate, which is the realized average of the CBOE 13-week T-bill yield (IRX) over the OOS window 2018-2024. The OOS period included near-zero rates 2018-2021 and 4-5% rates 2022-2024, so the 2.33% average is the appropriate within-sample anchor.

If a future report needs to use a different risk-free rate (e.g., a forward-looking expectation), the Sharpe will shift. The strategy’s annualized return is 2.41%, so the excess Sharpe is sensitive to the rf assumption: a rf of 2.0% would give Sharpe ≈ +0.95; a rf of 3.0% would give Sharpe ≈ -0.95. The strategy is barely-positive on excess basis in this rate regime.

3. OOS sample size

OOS spans 2018-01-01 to 2024-12-31, ≈ 7 years (1,760 trading days). Effective independent observations at the 30-day forward horizon (typical IC holding window) ≈ 84 non-overlapping windows. The volatility-risk-premium literature (Bollerslev/Tauchen/Zhou 2009) used 17 years of S&P data; ours is 7. The same caveat about limited post-1990 sample (per BTZ §3 footnote 23) applies amplified.

Strategy-level limitations

4. Tail risk is real and bounded but not zero

Put-credit-spread max-loss-per-spread is bounded by wing widths (5 strike points = $500 per spread per contract, before commissions). With realistic per-trade sizing (1% of equity per spread), absolute drawdown in a single day is bounded. But:

  • Multi-day cascade events (e.g., Volmageddon Feb 2018) can produce simultaneous emergency exits on multiple positions before the halt framework engages.
  • The 4-instrument basket’s correlation rises during stress events (typical ρ jump from 0.26 to 0.6+ in a crisis).

5. Strategy capacity is limited

OptionMetrics open-interest data shows our chosen 16-delta strikes have median per-strike OI in the hundreds-to-thousands for AAPL/MSFT/WMT and dozens-to-hundreds for GLD. Scaling beyond ~$5–20M of capital (depending on instrument) likely encounters meaningful market-impact friction beyond what our backtest’s bid-ask slippage model captures.

This is a retail / small-fund strategy, not an institutional one.

Methodological limitations

6. Iron-condor tested-and-rejected, not avoided

The pre-committed methodology specified iron condors (put + call wings). On the spot-corrected engine, iron condor variants underperformed put-only across every instrument tested. The headline is put-only; the iron-condor result is reported in Ablations as a tested extension with negative result. The decision to ship put-only is evidence-driven, but it differs from the original pre-commitment.

7. Daily-frequency RV components in the realized-vol indicators

Per BTZ 2009 §3.2.1: 5-minute intraday squared returns produce stronger predictability for VRP-based forecasting than daily close-to-close RV. We use daily RV in the engine’s vol-multiplier computation. Without intraday data, we cannot match the BTZ-preferred specification.

8. R² inflation per Boudoukh-Richardson-Whitelaw (2008)

In overlapping multi-period predictive regressions, R² inflates roughly proportionally with horizon × overlap, even in the absence of true predictability. Our 30-day forward window with daily observations creates this pattern.

We mitigate by reporting Hodrick (1992) standard errors (src/metrics/hodrick_se.py) as the primary significance measure for any regression we report, rather than naive Newey-West. R² is reported descriptively only.

Backtest-environment limitations

9. Synthetic-pricing fallback when OptionMetrics IV missing

OptionMetrics has ~7-15% NaN rate on delta and IV per ticker (concentrated at deep-ITM/OTM strikes where Greeks aren’t computed). Our engine falls back to BS-derived deltas using VIX/100 as ATM-IV proxy when needed. This is a known approximation; magnitude is small at our 16-delta strikes (which have populated deltas in the OptionMetrics data) but worth flagging.

10. Friction-model calibration is best-effort

Slippage, commission, and gap-aware-fill parameters are pre-committed but not directly calibrated to specific broker fee schedules. Real-world results will differ in these dimensions. We err conservative (more slippage in higher-VIX buckets, gap-fills at worst execution).

11. American early-exercise risk on dividend-paying single-stocks

Per Merton (1973) Theorem 13, American puts on dividend-paying stocks have positive probability of premature exercise. Our headline basket has 3 of 4 names paying dividends (AAPL, MSFT, WMT, GLD is a commodity ETF and does not pay dividends).

Pricing approximation: we use BS European-style for all wheel names. This undervalues the option for the counterparty (we underestimate the premium they’d rationally demand). Magnitude is small at our 16-delta OTM entry strikes; grows when underlying drops materially and put becomes ITM near ex-dividend dates. We do not model the assignment cash-flow precisely; reported P&L represents OTM-expiration economics.

12. BS pricing biases (the source of VRP itself)

Per Black & Scholes (1973) page 654 empirical test on 545 OTC option contracts: BS systematically overvalues options on high-variance underlyings and undervalues low-variance. This bias is the structural source of the volatility risk premium we harvest. We do not “out-predict” BS; we systematically take the side of the trade where empirical VRP is positive in the historical record. The strategy’s edge is contingent on this bias persisting in future periods.

Exogenous-factor data limitations

13. TWS-sourced macro factors

The exogenous factors used by the engine (VIX, VIX3M, VVIX, SKEW, IRX, TNX, HYG, LQD, SPY, SPX bars) are sourced from the Interactive Brokers TWS feed snapshot frozen at commit time. These are point-in-time by the snapshot date but lack the explicit “as-of-decision-time” stamping that institutional data providers (Bloomberg, Refinitiv) provide. We use pd.Series.asof(today) to enforce no-look-ahead, but small backfill artifacts in the source feed are possible.

14. Per-ticker underlying spot from OptionMetrics IvyDB Securities

The 4-instrument basket’s underlying daily spots (AAPL, MSFT, WMT, GLD) are sourced from the OptionMetrics IvyDB Securities file (queried via WRDS). This is the same vendor as the option chain data, which ensures secid alignment across the chain and the underlying. Daily close prices match published exchange records on spot-checked reference dates (e.g., AAPL 2018-04-16 = $175.82, matches exchange record).

Live monitoring limitations

15. Hoeffding bound is worst-case

The bound holds for any bounded RV regardless of distribution, but it is loose. Tighter bounds exist (e.g., Bernstein, normal-approximation) for distributions where assumptions hold. We use Hoeffding for distribution-free guarantees; this means the 50/25/10% thresholds are conservative, the actual probability of regime change is at most these values, not exactly these values.

16. Pre-committed μ does not adapt over time

The committed μ baseline is computed from the OOS backtest and held fixed in live deployment. If we adapted μ live (e.g., updating to the rolling 12-month observed win rate), we would lose the pre-commitment property and the framework would become a moving-goalpost circular reference. Trade-off: occasional false-negatives in genuine regime changes that look “normal” relative to the pre-committed baseline.

Multi-testing limitations beyond the headline

17. Mode comparison creates a second multiple-testing layer

The ablation includes mode comparisons (naked vs halts_only). Comparing the better of the two modes against the anchor introduces a second selection layer beyond the basket-selection layer corrected via DSR + PBO.

The headline only reports the halts_only mode result. The naked-mode results are reported in the ablation pages for transparency, not as candidates.
